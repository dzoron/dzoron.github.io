01  THE YOUNG WARLOCK

(Dark cinematic folk-rock / industrial blues undertone. Starts intimate with acoustic guitar, floor-tom and low cello. Gradually reveals distorted bass, strange mechanical percussion, and a distant choir. The music should sound like an old folk tale discovering machinery underneath itself.)

(Intro: single acoustic guitar, faint ticking somewhere behind it)

They called the boy a warlock
when the trick worked twice.
He laughed.
There was no spell.
He had just watched
where everyone else stopped looking.

(Verse 1)

He learned the hinge by breaking doors,
learned the rain from rotten floors,
learned that every miracle
had fingerprints and hidden cords.
He watched the candle climb the air,
watched the room pretend to stare,
found the heat beneath the wonder,
found the little roadway there.
No prophecy beneath his skin.
No ancient bloodline calling him.
Just too many questions in his head
and nowhere else to put them.

(Pre-Chorus)

More room
meant more doors.
More doors
meant more wrong turns.
More wrong turns
meant learning
what the fire burns.

(Chorus)

They called it magic.
He called it process.
They called him gifted.
He called it cost.
Every answer opened sideways.
Every road exposed another cross.
He wasn't chosen.
He was looking.
And looking has a price.
The world keeps giving up its secrets
when you ask the question twice.

(Verse 2)

He learned the scar by missing turns,
learned the map from what still burns,
learned that every broken answer
leaves a shape when it returns.
He learned the edge by pushing walls,
learned the weight from watching falls,
found that every hidden structure
leaves a shadow when it calls.
No single frame could hold the same.
Too many roads. Too many names.
So he built a place where all of them
could stand without becoming one.

(Pre-Chorus)

More roads
meant more crossings.
More crossings
meant more seams.
More seams
meant building
somewhere big enough
to dream.

(Chorus)

They called it magic.
He called it process.
They called him gifted.
He called it cost.
Every answer opened sideways.
Every road exposed another cross.
He wasn't chosen.
He was looking.
And looking has a price.
The world keeps giving up its secrets
when you ask the question twice.

(Bridge: the established form fractures; ticking, voice, low strings)

So he made a universe.
Not to escape this one.
To arrange it.
Put consequence beside choice.
Put memory beside change.
Put monsters where monsters belonged.
Put gods where gods could be questioned.
Put roads between things
that had never been allowed to touch.
And somewhere between
the first border
and the first crossing,
he stopped writing a world
and started drawing
the shape of how he thought.

(Silence)

He did not notice.
Not yet.

(Instrumental turn: the original acoustic pattern returns beneath the machinery)

(Verse 3: same structure as Verse 1, but the language has changed)

He learned the border drawing lands,
learned the path by moving hands,
learned that every invented kingdom
still obeyed what made it stand.
He watched the rivers choose their beds,
watched the causes pull their threads,
found the shapes beneath the stories,
found the roads inside his head.
No prophecy had brought him here.
No hidden voice had made it clear.
He had followed one small process
till the pattern could appear.

(Pre-Chorus: exact musical return)

More room
meant more doors.
More doors
meant more wrong turns.
More wrong turns
meant learning
what the fire burns.

(Final Chorus: original structure, expanded only at the final turn)

They called it magic.
He called it process.
They called him gifted.
He called it cost.
Every answer opened sideways.
Every road exposed another cross.
He wasn't chosen.
He was looking.
And looking changed his eyes.
The world kept giving up its secrets
when he asked the question twice.
They called it fiction.
He called it stories.
He thought the borders
still were real.
But every world he built from nothing
carried something he could feel.
He wasn't chosen.
He was looking.
And somewhere in the night,
the shape he'd drawn to hold the world
began
to look
back.

(Outro: return to the exact intimacy of the Intro. Guitar and ticking only.)

They called the man a warlock
when the pattern moved.
He laughed.
There was no spell.
He had just followed
where everyone else stopped looking.

(soft mechanical click)

Something in the static
answered.
