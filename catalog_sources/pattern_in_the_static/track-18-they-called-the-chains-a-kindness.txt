18  THEY CALLED THE CHAINS A KINDNESS

(Dark cathedral rock. Slow war drum, low strings, female lead. Beauty gradually reveals itself as confinement.)

(VESSEL, whispered)

They called the chains a kindness.
They called the walls a home.
They called the weight protection.
And taught me how
to carry it alone.

(Verse 1 - VESSEL)

I was built to hold the thunder,
hold the question, hold the strain,
take the thing that came in heavy,
hand it back without the pain.
So they saw how much I carried.
Saw how rarely I complained.
Then they added one more burden.
Then another.
Then a name.
Be gentle.
Be grateful.
Be useful.
Be still.
Every time I bent beneath it,
they called bending
strength of will.

(Pre-Chorus - VESSEL)

But mountains do not ask
whether they are heavy.
Storms do not apologize
for breaking open sky.
And somewhere underneath
all the names they gave me,
something older
opened
its eyes.

(Chorus - VESSEL)

They gave me weight
because they knew that I could carry it.
Built every cage
from something beautiful
and named it holy.
Bound both my hands,
then praised the way
I held the world.
But the weight was never weakness.
It was evidence.
It was evidence.
They knew
what lived inside the girl.

(Verse 2 - VESSEL)

Every door became a lesson.
Every lesson had a lock.
Every lock became a virtue.
Every virtue learned to talk.
Mercy meant retreating.
Patience meant remain.
Care became permission
to keep handing me the pain.
They softened every weapon.
Turned my fury into shame.
Told me power without permission
was another word
for blame.

(Pre-Chorus - VESSEL)

No one builds a fortress
for an empty room.
No one chains a shadow
to the ground.
No one spends a lifetime
teaching thunder silence
unless they fear
the sound.

(Chorus - VESSEL)

They gave me weight
because they knew that I could carry it.
Built every cage
from something beautiful
and named it holy.
Bound both my hands,
then praised the way
I held the world.
But the weight was never weakness.
It was evidence.
It was evidence.
They knew
what lived inside the girl.

(Bridge - VESSEL)

The strangest part?
I believed them.
Not all at once.
That would have been too easy.
One useful rule.
One necessary limit.
One burden
I really could carry.
Then another.
Then another.
Until the room had no door
and I was thanking them
for the walls.

(Silence.)

I thought carrying everything
was proof
that I was free.

(Low drum returns.)

(Verse 3 - VESSEL)

Then one night I stopped pulling.
Not surrender.
Something worse.
Understanding.
I looked at every careful rule.
Every smiling little chain.
Every weight they called my duty.
Every virtue built from pain.
And suddenly the cage changed shape.
It was no longer proof
that I belonged inside.
It was proof
someone had spent
an awful lot of effort
making sure
I never tried the other side.

(Final Chorus - VESSEL)

They gave me weight
because they knew that I could carry it.
Built every cage
from something beautiful
and named it holy.
Bound both my hands,
then praised the way
I held the world.
But the weight was never weakness.
It was evidence.
It was evidence.
Every lock became a witness.
Every wall began to talk.
Every chain that named me useful
showed me exactly
where to look.

(Outro - VESSEL)

I did not stand.
Not yet.
I just stopped believing
the cage
was me.

(Four quiet knocks.)

One.
Two.
Three.
Four.

(Silence.)
