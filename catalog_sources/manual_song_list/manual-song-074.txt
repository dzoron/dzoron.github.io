It Was Evidence - Luna for Vessel 

(Intro: cathedral bell, low war drum, distant choir; a single female voice almost whispered)

They called the chains a kindness.

They called the walls a home.

They called the weight protection.

And taught her how to kneel beneath it.

(Verse 1: slow, cinematic, dark orchestral pulse)
She was born with thunder in her bones,
Fire sleeping underneath her tongue,
Hands that could have lifted kingdoms,
So they taught her she was young.

Wrapped her wrists in golden mercy,
Hung obedience from her spine,
Said, “A hero knows her limits.”

Then they drew the limits twice.

Every door became a lesson.
Every lesson had a lock.
Every lock became a virtue.
Every virtue learned to talk.

“Be gentle.”
“Be grateful.”
“Be useful.”
“Be still.”

And every time she bent beneath them,
They called bending strength of will.

(Pre-Chorus: strings climbing, drums waking)
But mountains do not ask
whether they are heavy.

Storms do not apologize
for breaking open sky.

And somewhere underneath
all the names they gave her,

something older
opened its eyes.

(Chorus: huge cinematic rock hit)
They gave her weight
because they knew that she could carry it.

Built every cage
from something beautiful and named it holy.

Bound both her hands,
then praised the way she held the world.

But the weight was never weakness.

It was evidence.

It was evidence.

They knew what lived inside the girl.

(Verse 2: heavier, darker; toms and brass)
They feared the day she learned the difference
between mercy and retreat,
Between choosing not to strike
and being taught she had no teeth.

So they softened every weapon.
Turned her fury into shame.
Told her power without permission
was another word for blame.

They dressed the guards like healers.
They dressed the walls like grace.
They taught her every smiling chain
was there to keep her safe.

Still she heard the buildings trembling
every time she crossed the floor.

Not because she broke the structure.

Because it knew
she could.

(Pre-Chorus: low choir joins)
No one builds a fortress
for an empty room.

No one chains a shadow
to the ground.

No one spends a lifetime
teaching thunder silence

unless they fear
the sound.

(Chorus: bigger)
They gave her weight
because they knew that she could carry it.

Built every cage
from something beautiful and named it holy.

Bound both her hands,
then praised the way she held the world.

But the weight was never weakness.

It was evidence.

It was evidence.

They knew what lived inside the girl.

(Bridge: music drops to piano, heartbeat drum; intimate vocal)
One night
she stopped pulling at the chains.

Not surrender.

Something worse.

Understanding.

She looked at the iron.

Looked at the walls.

Looked at the thousand careful rules
written by people
who had never carried what she carried.

Then she laughed.

Very softly.

Because suddenly
the cage had changed shape.

It was no longer proof
that she belonged inside.

It was proof
they had been afraid
to see her outside.

(Build: low strings, heartbeat accelerating)
She did not become cruel.

She did not become wild.

She did not burn the city
just to prove she could survive.

She simply stood.

And every weight
that had been called her duty

became a thing
she was allowed
to put down.

(Breakdown: massive drums, distorted guitars, choir chanting)
DROP THE CROWN.

DROP THE CHAIN.

DROP THE VIRTUE
BUILT FROM PAIN.

DROP THE HALO.

DROP THE NAME.

LET THEM LEARN
WHO BORE THE WEIGHT.

(Sudden silence)

She rose.

Not lighter.

Stronger.

(Final Chorus: maximal, heroic, dark, brass and choir)
They gave her weight
because they knew that she could carry it.

Built every cage
from something beautiful and named it holy.

Bound both her hands,
then praised the way she held the world.

Now every lock is testimony.

Every scar a map.

Every wall confession.

Every chain an answered fact.

The weight was never weakness.

It was evidence.

It was evidence.

They knew exactly
what she was.

(Outro: choir fades; single low female voice)

And when they asked
what power finally set her free,

she smiled.

“Choice.”

(blackout)
