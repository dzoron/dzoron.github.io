Selling Blue - Denial in the Static - Daniel and Luna

[Intro: free time, piano only]

There they go again.

Selling weather
from a room
with no windows.

[Verse 1: uneven waltz, clipped delivery]

Paper boats.

Perfect coats.

Little captains
clearing throats.

A painted wave
held overhead.

“Terrible ocean,”
someone said.

Someone nodded.

Someone knew.

Someone had a picture too.

Blue on blue
with teeth beneath.

A borrowed storm
in borrowed speech.

And me?

Salt cuff.

Wet shoes.

Quietly counting
the missing screws.

[Lift: drums begin to hurry]

They have maps
with nowhere on them.

They have bells
that never rang.

They have warnings
copied carefully

from the mouth
that copied
the mouth
that sang.

[Chorus: wide 4/4, long phrases]

Let them name the ocean
from the postcards in their hands.

Let them draw enormous monsters
on their clean and distant sands.

I have slept below the water.

I have heard the old hull groan.

But a room that came for thunder
does not want the undertow.

So I smile.

I nod.

I let the little lighthouse turn.

While every timber in their story
splits and buckles in my head.

I could dismantle every sentence.

I could show them where it bled.

Instead I say,

“Mm.”

And let the painted water spread.

[Verse 2: bass-led, short lines against longer instrumental bars]

Now the sky
is in a bottle.

Now the moon
has joined a gang.

Now the staircase
keeps a ledger

of the feet
that made it angry.

Spoons are plotting.

Curtains lie.

Every clock
has learned to spy.

A gull repeats it.

Three gulls agree.

Consensus blooms
beside the sea.

They build a mast
from matchstick facts.

Hang velvet sails
on thumbtack maps.

Then ask me why
I’m looking down.

There’s a leak
beneath the crown.

[Lift: faster, almost tumbling]

Pass it left.

Polish it bright.

Remove the doubt.

Increase the fright.

Add one wolf.

Subtract the frame.

Give the fog
a proper name.

[Chorus: same broad melody, altered ending]

Let them name the ocean
from the postcards in their hands.

Let them chart invented shipwrecks
on invented strips of land.

I could tell them where the reef sleeps.

I could tell them why it formed.

But a room that loves the warning
rarely wants to meet the storm.

So I smile.

I wait.

I let the little compass spin.

While every hinge inside their argument
comes quietly undone.

I could place the pieces on the table.

I could number every one.

Instead I say,

“Mm.”

Because the evening has just begun.

[Bridge: rhythm changes repeatedly, each section shorter]

[Slow]

It happens in the chapel.

[Quicker]

It happens in the lab.

[Quicker]

It happens when a clever crowd
discovers the approved new flag.

[Half-time]

It happens over coffee.

In offices.

In cars.

People drawing constellations
from reflections
on parked cars.

[Silence, then spoken]

And I have done it too.

Swallowed painted rain.

Mistook an echo
for a door.

Mistook repetition
for a chain.

But once you’ve tasted river water,

you know when someone’s selling blue.

[Instrumental break: piano and brushed snare, no fixed bar length]

[Breakdown: dry spoken rhythm]

The wolves are voting.

The lamps are sad.

The mirror has a childhood.

The kettle’s turning bad.

The carpet fears replacement.

The ceiling knows your name.

The spoon has found a microphone.

The microphone found flame.

Share it.

Trim it.

Caption it.

Again.

Nothing crosses oceans faster

than a sentence
with no chain.

[Final Chorus: begins softly, builds late]

Let them name the ocean
from whatever washed ashore.

Let them hold a shell against the ear
and call the whisper war.

I could open every engine.

I could lay the gears in rows.

But knowing how a story fails
doesn’t mean the room must know.

So I keep the salt upon me.

I keep the depth below.

I keep the thousand quiet answers
that would have nowhere useful to go.

And while they sail their paper kingdom
toward a cardboard setting sun,

I lift my glass.

I watch it pass.

And say,

“Mm.”

Just one.

[Outro: free time]

They ask whether the ocean
is becoming dangerous.

I look at the tide.

Then at the postcard.

Then back at the tide.

“Mm.”
