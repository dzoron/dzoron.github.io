Song Behind the Wall - Luna and Daniel 

(Intro: faint piano through stone, almost inaudible; low mechanical pulse beneath it)

He had mapped every corridor.

Every lock.

Every lie.

Then one night...

the wall sang back.

(Verse 1: restrained dark cabaret, male vocal)
It started with a single note
Where no note ought to be,
Behind a wall he'd measured twice
And marked as solid masonry.

Not a warning.
Not a bell.
Not another hidden test.

Just four quiet notes repeating
Like a heartbeat under dress.

He pressed his ear against the stone.
The melody withdrew.

He stepped away.

It came again.

One.

Two.

Three.

Then two.

(Pre-Chorus: bass enters, tension slowly growing)
Every rule had left a footprint.
Every lock had shown a seam.

But this...

This wasn't architecture.

This was something trying to sing.

(Chorus: wide cinematic hit, brass and guitars restrained)
There was a song behind the wall,
Too small to break it,
Too stubborn to fall.

No door.
No name.
No face at all.

Just a song
behind the wall.

And every time the room went still,
it rose again against its will.

Not loud enough to conquer stone.

Just loud enough
to not be gone.

(Verse 2: rhythm develops, female harmony barely audible underneath)
He tried the tricks he'd learned before.
Pressure.
Silence.
Tone.

But every clever little method
left the melody alone.

Push the wall,
the song grew softer.

Call aloud,
the rhythm fled.

So he did the strangest thing
he'd learned since entering:

He listened instead.

The melody returned.

Not where he'd heard it.

Somewhere farther down the hall.

Then beneath him.

Then above.

Then somewhere
inside them all.

(Pre-Chorus: female voice begins answering phrases)
He followed rests instead of footsteps.

Followed rhythm instead of signs.

Found a stair behind a silence
that existed between lines.

(Chorus: female voice now clearly harmonizing)
There was a song behind the wall,
Too small to break it,
Too stubborn to fall.

No door.
No name.
No face at all.

Just a song
behind the wall.

And every time the room went still,
it rose again against its will.

Not loud enough to conquer stone.

Just loud enough
to not be gone.

(Bridge: drums vanish; male spoken vocal over distant strings)
At the bottom of the stair
was a room
that wasn't on his map.

Of course.

He almost laughed.

There was no lock.

No guard.

No chain.

Only a woman
sitting beneath a dead chandelier,

tapping four beats
against her knee.

One.
Two.
Three.
Four.

Pause.

One.
Two.
Three.
Four.

He asked,

“Are you trapped here?”

She looked at him
like he'd asked whether moonlight
was trapped inside the night.

“No.”

“Then why don't you leave?”

She smiled.

“I keep forgetting the way.”

(Piano enters)

“So I leave myself music.”

(Build: female vocal takes lead for first time)
A rhythm in the floorboards.
A rhyme beneath the door.
A melody for finding
what I came here looking for.

If the hallway changes places,
if the stair forgets its name,
I leave something I can follow
when I do not feel the same.

(Male vocal)
“I mapped the walls.”

(Female vocal, amused)
“I mapped the echoes.”

(Male)
“I learned where every doorway hides.”

(Female)
“I learned what always comes back.”

Together:
“Then perhaps we know the room
from different sides.”

(Breakdown: heavy drums arrive, first real power)
MAP THE STONE.

KEEP THE SONG.

MARK THE PLACE
WHERE THINGS GO WRONG.

IF THE ROAD
FORGETS THE WAY,

LEAVE A RHYTHM.

LET IT STAY.

MAP THE LOCK.

KEEP THE KEY.

BUT LEAVE A NOTE
FOR WHO YOU'LL BE.

WHEN THE ROOM
REWRITES THE DARK,

FOLLOW MUSIC.

FIND THE MARK.

(Swing Break: lively brass appears for first time, Luna beginning to emerge)
She tapped the table.

One, two, three.

He heard the hidden symmetry.

Four steps forward.
One step wrong.
Turn the error
into song.

Every hallway had a meter.
Every warning had a rhyme.

She had never learned the building.

She had learned
how to survive
when it changed its mind.

(Final Chorus: female lead now dominant, full cinematic cabaret)
There was a song behind the wall,
Too strange to own,
Too bright to fall.

No crown.
No cage.
No grand recall.

Just a song
behind the wall.

And every time the world went still,
she left a rhythm stronger still.

Not meant to conquer.
Not command.

Just something she could understand.

There was a song behind the wall.

Then there was a voice.

Then there was laughter.

Then there was a woman
standing underneath the broken chandelier
with music in her hands.

(Outro: instruments disappear until only four taps remain)

The man asked,

“What should I call you?”

(long pause)

She considered this very seriously.

Then smiled.

“Luna.”

Four taps.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

(whisper)

“If the road moves again...

I'll write another song.”
