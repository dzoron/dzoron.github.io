LAUGH FROM BELOW

(Industrial electro-swing / infernal orchestral metal. Crooked piano, upright bass, brass choir, distorted strings, heavy mechanical drums. Male lead with sly theatrical swagger; female backing vocal occasionally needles him from the wings. Dark, funny, dangerous without becoming grandiose.)

(Intro: low organ, glass clink, four taps from somewhere beneath the stage)

They built a staircase.

Very impressive.

Marble. Gold. Excellent acoustics.

(pause)

I took the service entrance.

(brass snort)

(Verse 1: slow swing)

I've got a little fire in every hand, a dozen doors nobody understands, names in books that shouldn't rhyme, knives made out of borrowed time.

I know the words that make rooms bend. I know how far a signal sends. I know exactly what I could become

if I mistook ability for permission.

(Pre-Chorus: strings creep upward)

They point above.

“Ascend.”

They say.

“You've earned the height.”

I look at all those lovely stairs

and ask,

“Earned the right to what?”

(Chorus: huge brass + industrial guitars)

I LAUGH FROM BELOW.

Not because I cannot climb.

I know where every stair goes.

I just don't confuse height with mine.

I LAUGH FROM BELOW.

Let the kings keep counting crowns.

If power needs me over you,

I'll stay beneath and burn it down.

Not myself.

The pedestal.

Learn the difference.

(Post-Chorus: clown choir)

HE COULD ASCEND!

(female voice)

He heard you.

(kazoo)

(Verse 2: faster electro-swing)

They say,

“You waste what you possess.”

What a fascinating confession.

Apparently every loaded gun must become somebody's lesson.

Every key must turn a lock. Every hand must move a block. Every mind that sees the mechanism

owes the room an intervention.

No.

Capability is not debt.

I wrote that somewhere.

Probably in fire.

(Pre-Chorus)

If I can lift ten thousand pounds, does every weight become my ground?

If I can see another path, does every traveler owe me maps?

If I can open every door,

am I required to walk through yours?

(Chorus: heavier)

I LAUGH FROM BELOW.

Not because I fear the sky.

I've seen what happens to a man who starts believing altitude means “why.”

I LAUGH FROM BELOW.

Let the prophets stare me down.

The easiest way to lose your name

is turning it into a crown.

(Bridge: orchestra strips down to piano and cello)

And yes...

there is a darker temptation.

Not the throne.

The view.

To stand above the room and finally see everyone small.

To look at every hand that called restraint weakness and laugh.

(silence)

That laugh is easy.

Too easy.

(soft female backing vocal)

So where do you laugh?

(male lead, almost smiling)

Down here.

Where they can still laugh back.

(industrial pulse returns)

(Verse 3: crooked swagger)

I don't kneel to make them taller.

I don't shrink to make them right.

I just refuse to turn my sightline into evidence of right.

Below is not beneath in worth.

Above is not a second birth.

Sometimes the lower floor's the only place

where everyone still has a face.

(Pre-Chorus: rising brass)

Let someone else announce the age.

Let someone else demand the stage.

I'll keep the trapdoor well maintained

and ruin every sacred claim.

(Breakdown: massive half-time stomp)

I COULD TAKE THE THRONE.

THAT DOESN'T MAKE IT MINE.

I COULD CLOSE THE GATE.

THAT DOESN'T MAKE IT RIGHT.

I COULD WRITE THE RULE.

THAT DOESN'T MAKE YOU SUBJECT.

I COULD WIN THE ROOM.

(beat drops)

I'D RATHER KEEP THE EXIT.

(choir)

ASCEND!

(Daniel)

No.

(choir, confused)

ASCEND!

(Daniel)

Still no.

(female voice, laughing)

He's very difficult.

(Daniel)

Thank you.

(Final Chorus: full orchestra, industrial wall, brass screaming)

I LAUGH FROM BELOW.

Where the dangerous choices live.

Where power means the things I could demand

and character means what I refuse to take.

I LAUGH FROM BELOW.

Not lesser.

Not above.

Not hiding from the height.

Just standing where my hands can reach

without pretending reach is right.

If I ever climb, I'll climb because the work requires the view.

Not because the staircase whispered,

“Everyone beneath belongs to you.”

(Final bridge: sudden warm strings)

Maybe ascent was never altitude.

Maybe that was always the trick.

Maybe growing meant becoming harder

to corrupt with what I could do.

Maybe the warlock's greatest spell

was one he never cast.

Maybe sovereignty is knowing

which doors to leave unpassed.

(Outro: crooked piano, four taps below the stage)

One.

Power.

Two.

Permission.

Three.

Restraint.

(long silence)

Four.

Choice.

(glass clink)

They wanted me above them.

I wanted us still able to laugh.

So I stayed where the joke could reach everyone.
